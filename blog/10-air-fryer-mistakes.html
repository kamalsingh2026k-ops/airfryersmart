10 Air Fryer Mistakes Beginners Make (And the Fixes)

2026-08-16 · 8 min read · AirFryerSmart editorial team

Most air fryer disappointment is not the appliance's fault. It is one of a small set of recurring mistakes, all of which have a physical explanation and a simple fix. Work through this list and your results will improve more than any gadget upgrade could achieve.

1. Overcrowding the basket

The mistake: loading the basket like a baking tray. Why it fails: an air fryer cooks by moving hot air around each piece of food. Every extra piece blocks someone else's airflow, and blocked airflow means steaming, not crisping. The food at the bottom of a full basket is effectively poaching in its own released moisture. The fix: cook in a single layer with a finger of space between pieces. For bigger batches, add about 13% more time for a large load, 25% for a family load — and accept that two batches always beat one full basket.

2. Not preheating

The mistake: putting food into a cold chamber. Why it fails: browning happens fastest when food meets hot metal and hot air immediately. A cold start means the surface slowly warms with the chamber, losing the early sear that builds crispness — especially on steak, chicken skin and frozen fries. The fix: run a 3-minute preheat for anything under 25 minutes of cooking. Skip it only for long cooks and reheating, where the difference is negligible.

3. Cooking wet food

The mistake: patting chicken wings dry is skipped; frozen food is thawed first. Why it fails: surface moisture must evaporate before browning can start, and evaporation is energy-expensive. Wet food steams, which is the direct opposite of what you want. Thawing breaded food is worse — water soaks into the coating and it never crisps again. The fix: pat protein thoroughly dry before seasoning. Breaded and par-fried frozen food goes straight from the freezer to the basket. Vegetables get a light oil coat so their moisture does not sit on the surface.

4. Using aerosol cooking spray

The mistake: spraying the basket with canola oil from a pressurized can. Why it fails: the propellant and emulsifiers in aerosol sprays attack non-stick coatings. Over months of use, the coating degrades and food starts sticking — the damage is cumulative and invisible until it is done. The fix: use a pump mister with your own oil, or brush oil on with a silicone brush. Your basket will outlive the one owned by the person who sprays it.

5. Skipping the shake

The mistake: starting the timer and walking away. Why it fails: the food sitting against the basket floor stays pale, and pieces that started on top finish on top. An unshaken basket gives you half-crisp food and, with fries, welded-together clumps. The fix: shake at the halfway point — for most foods, around the times listed in the food database. Use tongs for anything breaded, which shakes apart.

6. Saucing too early

The mistake: tossing wings in BBQ or honey sauce before cooking. Why it fails: sugar burns at air fryer temperatures. Sauce applied early caramelizes, then scorches, then glues the food to the basket — all while adding zero crispness. The fix: cook first, sauce after, or apply in the final two to three minutes only. The exception that proves the rule: glazes brushed on in the last minutes are exactly right.

7. Cooking without a thermometer

The mistake: judging doneness by time and appearance. Why it fails: the air fryer's whole trick is browning the outside quickly, so food can look finished several minutes before the centre is safe. Chicken browns beautifully at 155°F — 10°F short of the 165°F (74°C) USDA minimum for poultry. The fix: use an instant-read thermometer. Ground meats need 160°F (71°C), whole cuts 145°F (63°C) plus a 3-minute rest, fish 145°F (63°C).

8. Blocking the airflow with liners

The mistake: lining the basket with solid parchment or foil. Why it fails: a solid sheet converts the basket into a steaming tray. The fan pushes air across the top of the food and the liner holds the moisture in underneath — soggy bases, every time. The fix: use perforated liners, or no liner at all and just wash the basket. If you must use foil, keep it flat, weighted by food, and never covering the air gaps at the basket's edges.

9. Light food flying into the element

The mistake: air frying kale, sandwich bread or sliced salami and finding charred debris on the heating element. Why it fails: the fan is strong enough to lift light, dry pieces into the element, where they smoke and scorch. The fix: weigh light food down with a metal rack or trivet placed on top, or weigh it down with heavier food. Never leave empty parchment in a preheating air fryer for the same reason.

10. Never cleaning the element

The mistake: washing the basket, ignoring everything else. Why it fails: grease accumulates on the heating element and the surrounding surfaces. It burns at cooking temperatures, producing smoke and an acrid taste that migrates into every meal. It also slowly reduces heating efficiency. The fix: wipe the element and interior weekly with a damp cloth when cool, and give the machine a proper clean monthly. Full instructions in our cleaning guide.

One bonus habit

Keep the printable cheat sheet on the fridge for the first month. Most of these mistakes stop happening once the correct numbers are where you can actually see them mid-cook — which is also why the conversion calculator shows its working instead of just spitting out a number.

The bottom line

None of these fixes require skill or practice — they are procedural. Single layer, preheat, dry food, shake halfway, correct liner, thermometer, clean element. Do those seven things consistently and the air fryer will make you look like a much better cook than you are.
