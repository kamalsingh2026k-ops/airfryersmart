The Air Fryer Frozen Food Guide: Times, Temperatures and Rules

2026-08-16 · 8 min read · AirFryerSmart editorial team

Frozen food is where the air fryer earns its keep. French fries, nuggets, fish sticks, wings, mozzarella sticks, egg rolls — all of these are par-fried or par-baked at the factory and then frozen. The air fryer's entire job is to re-crisp them, which is exactly what a small, fast-moving convection chamber does best. No oven preheat, no oil, no thawing, and a result closer to the fryer than the oven ever manages.

Rule one: never thaw breaded or par-fried food

This is the most counter-intuitive and most important rule. Thawing releases water into the coating. That water then steams instead of crisps, and once a breaded coating has absorbed moisture it never comes back. Frozen nuggets, fries, fish sticks, mozzarella sticks and onion rings go straight from the freezer to the basket.

The one exception is plain protein — raw frozen chicken breast or salmon — which cooks perfectly from frozen, but at a lower temperature (about 15-25°F below the fresh setting) so the centre reaches doneness before the outside dries out. Frozen chicken wings cook fine from frozen at the same temperature as fresh, just longer.

Rule two: add roughly 35% more time

If you know the fresh cooking time, add about 35%. In practice that lands in a 5-15 minute band, depending on how long the cook was to begin with. A 10-minute fresh cook becomes roughly 14; a 20-minute cook becomes 27. Our conversion calculator applies this automatically when you toggle "Cooking from frozen".

Rule three: shake more, not less

Frozen pieces start stuck together — fries clump, wings freeze into a block, dumplings weld to each other. Shake at the halfway mark, and for anything in a solid block, separate the pieces at the 6-8 minute mark once the ice glue releases. A stuck-together batch steams at every point of contact.

Frozen protein, the right way

Frozen chicken breast: 360°F (182°C) for about 28 minutes, flipping at 15, to 165°F (74°C) internal. Frozen salmon: 375°F (191°C) for about 14 minutes. Frozen shrimp: 400°F (200°C) for about 9 minutes, shaking twice, done when opaque and C-shaped. The principle for all three: lower temperature than fresh, longer time, and a thermometer rather than a clock.

Reheating leftovers is the same system

Leftover pizza, fries and fried chicken all reheat beautifully in an air fryer because reheating is just re-crisping. The numbers are much shorter than frozen cooking because the food starts cooked:

  • Leftover pizza: 350°F (177°C) for 3-4 minutes, slices flat, not overlapping.
  • Leftover fries: 400°F (200°C) for 3 minutes, one shake.
  • Leftover fried chicken: 380°F (193°C) for 4-5 minutes.
  • Leftover roasted vegetables: 350°F (177°C) for 3 minutes.

USDA guidance applies here too: reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C) throughout. With these foods, that happens well before they are re-crisped, so the visual cue and the thermometer agree.

Why it works, in one sentence

Frozen breaded food is a dry, pre-fried shell around a cooked interior — exactly the two conditions hot dry moving air is best at finishing. That is why the same machine that crisps frozen fries also ruins wet batter: it needs the food to arrive dry. Keep it dry, keep it in one layer, keep it moving, and the freezer aisle becomes your best friend in the kitchen.

The bottom line

Frozen food in the air fryer follows three rules: never thaw breaded items, add about 35% time, and shake at halfway. For everything else, the calculator applies the frozen adjustment for you and the cheat sheet has all twelve of these timings on one printable page.
